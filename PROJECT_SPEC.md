# MBoard product specification

MBoard is a native, offline English and Burmese keyboard for iPhone and iPad running iOS or iPadOS 16 or later. The containing app provides keyboard setup, a practice field and links to support and privacy information. The UIKit keyboard extension handles typing in apps that permit third-party keyboards.

## Layout and editing

- Tap the MBoard logo to open a menu with Auto-Capitalization, Word Suggestions and learned-word clearing under Keyboard Settings, followed by Typing Tips. Its icon-free item reads Burmese Typing or English Typing for the active language. Auto-Capitalization opens a scrollable panel inside the keyboard. It explains sentence starts, shows a typing example under each choice and marks the saved choice with a checkmark. The choices form a grouped list with press highlighting. Tapping a row saves the choice immediately and moves the blue tint and filled checkmark to it. The panel stays open for further selections until Back returns to typing with the latest choice. Both actions preserve the host text, language and layer. Clearing history opens a confirmation submenu. The logo and hide button sit inward from the rounded top corners. The right-hand keyboard/down-arrow button hides the keyboard without clearing text.
- Typing Tips stays inside the keyboard. Back and Hide Keyboard share a fixed action row. The centered title scrolls with the cards, with 16 points before the first card and a small inset below the last. Burmese shows hold-and-release alternatives (`က` → `ဥ`) and explicit stacking (`က` + `္` + `က` → `က္က`), explaining that `္` is the alternative of `်`, available through Shift or a brief hold and release. Burmese prose uses taller line spacing. English shows Space cursor dragging and double-Space sentence endings. All emphasized example text, including keys and results, uses blue text without a box, border or tinted background. Tip cards have generous padding and separation; the portrait panel is taller for the longer Burmese explanations, and scrolls for compact heights and larger text. Returning restores the ordinary keyboard height, language and layer. Help never inserts demonstration text. No About or Privacy item appears in this menu.
- With Auto-Capitalization enabled in a prose field, a space, Return or punctuation after standalone English `i` capitalizes it to `I`. Turning automatic Shift off or choosing the quoted spelling preserves lowercase for that occurrence. Immediate Backspace after an automatic pronoun change removes the separator and restores lowercase. Deleting an automatically shifted initial gives the next letter lowercase by default.
- In ordinary text fields, starting a Burmese word immediately after an English word or an English word immediately after a Burmese word inserts one space. This applies to typed words and accepted suggestions, including Burmese input starting with ေ. Switching layouts alone does not insert text. Punctuation, digits and existing whitespace do not trigger this shortcut.
- On the Burmese base layer, holding a key and lifting in place chooses its Gboard default. Slide to select additional iOS Burmese compounds, including က္က and က္ခ on က, and all five န stacks on န. On top-row keys, added compounds appear above the original Gboard choices so existing horizontal slides keep their targets.
- Preserve the familiar Android Gboard phone key positions and language layers. Use the captured layouts for key placement and long presses. Use MyanSan Smart as a composition reference, with observed Gboard output taking priority when rules differ. [Burmese layout](references/gboard-burmese/README.md) · [English layout and emoji](references/gboard-english/README.md) · [Composition examples](references/myansan/README.md)
- The bottom-left language switch has a blue keycap with a centered destination label (`abc` or `ကခ`) on both letter and symbol pages. The adjacent symbol key uses the current language. Symbol pages stay open for consecutive symbols. After entering a symbol, Space or Return goes back to letters.
- Double-tapping Space after a word inserts the active language’s sentence ending and a space. Holding Space and dragging moves the caret. English Shift supports one-shot capitalization and Caps Lock. Auto-Capitalization offers Off, Sentence starts only, First English word after Burmese, and Every English word in mixed text. In sentence-mode fields, all three enabled styles capitalize sentence starts, including after `။`. After Burmese text, the three styles produce `ဒီနေ့ team meeting`, `ဒီနေ့ Team meeting` and `ဒီနေ့ Team Meeting`, respectively. The first English word is determined from the text at the caret. Decimal points inside numbers preserve the sentence context. English-only sentences use ordinary sentence capitalization. Every English Word in Mixed Text is the default and preserves the previous On behavior. An existing Off setting stays off. Enabled styles respect fields requesting no capitals, word capitals or all capitals, and skip automatic Shift for email and URL keyboards.
- Insert standard Unicode and preserve neighboring text and selections. Both က + ိ + ု and က + ု + ိ produce ကို. Burmese composition and deletion must account for scalar order, active syllables and host editing behavior. Invalidate stale composition after caret, selection or field changes.
- Use the system font and its Myanmar fallback. The host app controls the font used for inserted text. Capitalize English words in Burmese interface copy.

## Offline suggestions

- Show up to three suggestions in fixed columns. Place the first choice in the center, the second on the left and the third on the right. Preserve the tap target when additional results arrive. Corrections and word splits require an explicit tap.
- When English offers a correction, reserve the right-hand slot for the exact typed spelling in quotation marks. Tapping it confirms that occurrence without teaching a permanent spelling preference.
- English combines the available on-device English text checker with a bundled vocabulary and contraction list. Support prefix completion, spelling correction at the caret or inside a word, and missed spaces such as `thankyou`. Typing `Im` offers `I'm`. For ambiguous forms such as `well`, keep the contraction as an alternative. Space preserves the typed spelling.
- After accepting an English suggestion, including a phrase or contraction, at the end of ordinary text, the next English letter starts a new word. Punctuation attaches directly. Changing fields, moving the caret, opening the emoji picker or switching language clears pending spacing.
- Burmese uses conversational entries and 41,120 ICU word-list entries. Look for prefixes inside continuous text, avoid duplicating text to the right of the caret and preserve Burmese spacing. [Dictionary and license notices](THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md)
- Learn confirmed single-word suggestion taps in both languages. For English, also learn words completed through the keyboard’s own keys. Everyday words qualify after two uses and other words after three. Preserve deliberately entered capitalization and avoid promoting repeated common typos. A tap contributes a weight of 1 and a typed word contributes 0.3.
- Store up to 256 word records locally, with up to six preceding-word associations per English word. Weights halve every 30 days. Skipping suggestions in sensitive fields, forgetting a word, clearing history and disabling Word Suggestions apply to personal learning as described in the [privacy policy](PRIVACY.md).
- Keep key feedback and insertion responsive. Debounce lookup and discard stale results. Run dictionary indexing, candidate merging and history persistence off the input thread. UIKit text checking runs on its required main actor after the typing pause.

## Appearance and emoji

The keyboard uses the native iOS keyboard backdrop. Keys, suggestions, previews, alternate menus, typing tips and emoji inherit the appearance of the keyboard container and active field. iOS controls the outer corners and can leave extra space above the extension. The practice app follows the system appearance independently.

The emoji picker keeps one full scrollable grid. Up to 32 locally saved recents come first, followed by 144 curated everyday emoji and the rest of the catalog without duplicates. Keep iOS’s native glyphs and long-press skin-tone choices. The offline catalog uses Unicode Emoji 17.0 and CLDR 48 English and Burmese annotations, plus a small set of informal search aliases. Search matches whole words and prefixes, ranks exact terms before prefixes and common emoji before uncommon ties, and shows skin tones through long press instead of repeating every tone in ordinary results. Filter browsing, search, recents and variants to the emoji version supported by the installed iOS: 14.0 on iOS 16, 15.0 from 16.4, 15.1 from 17.4, 16.0 from 18.4 and 17.0 from 26.4.

Focusing search shows the typing keyboard with results above it, an “Emoji search” spacebar and a Done button that returns directly to host typing. Host text-input and selection callbacks exit search even when a tap reaffirms an unchanged caret in the same field. Also check the host context before routing a key, in case a host changes it without a callback. Hosts that report no interaction at all cannot expose an unchanged-caret tap to the extension; Done remains available. Inserting an emoji result keeps search open for additional emoji. Switching languages during search updates the return key.

## Validation and scope

Core regressions cover composition, deletion, replacement, spelling, personal ranking and persistence. The Burmese fixture includes 458 Gboard input sequences. Simulator UI tests exercise mixed-script spacing, compound selection, language switching, toolbar controls, suggestions and emoji in the practice field. Daily typing comfort and cross-app compatibility require physical-device testing. [Test instructions](CONTRIBUTING.md)

Typing requires no account, network or Full Access. iOS supplies its own keyboard in secure fields and some numeric fields, and apps can restrict third-party keyboards. Automatic correction, swipe typing, cloud sync and voice input are outside the current implementation. See the [changelog](CHANGELOG.md) for version status.
